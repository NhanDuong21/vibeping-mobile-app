# Mobile surface brief

- **Job and audience:** Operate mode for one person glancing at an iPhone while away from one Windows laptop. The first screen must answer whether VibePing is listening, whether Codex needs attention, and what remains cached when the laptop is unavailable.
- **Outcome and proof:** The user can trust the current connection state, see real activity and allowance data, enable notifications only after a tap, and recover from offline or stale states without technical language.
- **Selected direction:** Inherit “Quiet signal.” The first viewport is a calm status field with the current Codex state, allowance summary, and recent activity separated by rules rather than a dashboard card grid. The signal ring and one mint accent carry readiness; restrained amber/coral carry actionable exceptions.
- **Scope and boundaries:** Mobile-first Angular/Ionic PWA at 320–430 px, light/dark/system themes, Vietnamese copy, Tailwind utilities, local system type, sparse containers, and familiar bottom navigation. No chat, remote action, giant hero, decorative gradient, glass, fake chart, or nested card maze.
- **States and interaction:** First run, loading, empty, fresh, stale, offline, permission denied, stale phone registration, laptop stopped, update ready, long content, and safe unknown error. Controls are at least 44 px, focus is visible, permission follows a direct tap, feedback never claims physical delivery.
